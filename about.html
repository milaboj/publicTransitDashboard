Project Overview

This project explores public transit accessibility in Seattle by visualizing bus routes, light rail, transit stops, and walking access buffers. The goal is to better understand how transit availability varies across neighborhoods and how walkability affects access. The Seattle Public Transit Accessibility Dashboard explores how accessible public transportation is across Seattle neighborhoods. Using datasets from Seattle City GIS and King County Metro, the dashboard visualizes bus stops, light rail stations, and transit routes to help users understand how close different communities are to reliable transit. Interactive layers, buffer zones, and charts allow users to explore walkable access to transit and compare accessibility between neighborhoods. Our goal is to highlight patterns of transit connectivity and encourage conversations about mobility, equity, and urban planning. By making transit data easier to explore, this dashboard helps commuters, residents, and policymakers better understand how transportation infrastructure shapes everyday movement in Seattle.

How to Use the Map

Use the dashboard controls to toggle transit layers and walking buffers on and off. Comparing different walk times helps illustrate accessibility gaps and areas where transit access may be limited.

Why This Matters

Access to reliable public transit is a key equity issue. By visualizing walkable access to transit, this project highlights spatial differences that can inform transportation planning and policy decisions.
