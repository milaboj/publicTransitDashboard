Instructions

How to Use This Map This interactive dashboard is designed to help users explore public transit patterns and accessibility across the greater Seattle area. Follow the steps below to navigate and make the most of the map: Navigate the Map Use your mouse or trackpad to pan across the map and zoom in or out. This allows you to explore different neighborhoods and transit areas in more detail. Explore Layers Toggle different map layers on and off using the layer control panel. Each layer represents a different dataset, such as transit routes, neighborhoods , and light rail lines. Turning layers on and off help you focus on the different areas of transit and light rail stops within Seattle. Interact with Features Click on map elements(such as transit stops or routes) to view additional information. Pop-ups will display key details about each location. For transit stops, the pop-up includes an accessibility score ( 0 - 4 ) which would reflect how inclusive a specific stop is. This score is based on the availability of features such as the following: ADA Accessibility (for individuals with disabilities) Shelter Bike racks Score Meaning: 0 = Inadequate accessibility (No features available for accessibility) 1 = Okay (Few/ little to feature available for accessibility) 2 = Good accessibility (Scarce amount of accessibility) 3 = Greater accessibility (Most amount of features available within the area) 4 = High accessible (All key features are present) This scoring system helps users quickly compare stops and identify areas with stronger or weaker accessibility support. Interpret the Data The map visualizes transit infrastructure and usage patterns to support analysis and decision-making. Use the visual patterns like density and buffer zones to make insights about accessibility and connectivity. The dashboard is intended for students, community members, and researchers who are interested in understanding the relationship between transit stops and light rail stops, giving the idea of walking availability. Helping understand public transportation systems and the impact on mobility in the greater Seattle area.
